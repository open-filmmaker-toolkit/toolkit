Free tools for documentary and indie filmmakers

For the business side of making a film: budgets, releases, grant deadlines, call sheets, clearances, credits.

Each tool opens in your browser. There's nothing to install and no account to make. Your project is one file on your computer, and every tool reads the same file, so the people and places you add in one show up in the rest.

Start here

  1. Download the sample project, a made-up documentary called The River Keeps.
  2. Open any tool below and choose Open file to see it with real-looking data.
  3. When you start your own film, choose New project and save the file somewhere you'll find it again.

Working offline? Download the whole toolkit and open index.html.

How the tools connect

They all read the same project file, so what you do in one carries into the next. A few common paths:

  • Pitch the film

    Subject tracker → Treatment builder → Comps tracker → Pitch deck builder

    The people you’re following, your treatment and your comps all end up in the deck.

  • Raise the money

    Grant calendar → Grant narrative library → Funder CRM → Finance plan

    Deadlines, the answers you sent, and every funder conversation, adding up in the finance plan.

  • Budget and cash

    Budget → Budget converter → Finance plan → Cash flow

    Build the budget once, reshape it for each funder, and see when the money runs short.

  • Plan the shoot

    Location scout sheet → Interview prep → Shooting calendar → Call sheet → Release tracker

    Scout the places, prep the interviews, schedule the days, and start each day’s call sheet.

  • Hire the crew

    Deal memos → Budget → Call sheet → Credits builder

    The rate in a deal memo goes into the budget, and the credit you promised goes into the end credits.

  • Ready each day

    Shooting calendar → Gear list → COI and permit tracker → Call sheet

    Pack the gear for each day, and make sure the certificates and permits are in before you go.

  • Travel safely

    Shooting calendar → Travel sheet → Risk and hostile-environment plans → Security checklist

    One trip gets a travel sheet, a risk plan, and a checklist for your devices and footage.

  • From shoot to edit

    Camera and sound reports → Footage log → Interview log → Paper edit

    Log the cards, catalog the clips, mark the good moments, and turn transcripts into a paper edit.

  • Edit to lock

    Paper edit → Structure board → Cut version tracker → Clearance log → Music cue sheet

    Bites become scenes, screening notes stick to those scenes, and the locked cut’s details go where they’re needed.

  • Track the spending

    Budget → Expense log → Budget-to-actuals

    Log what you spend as you go, and see which lines are running over.

  • Clear the film

    Research and archival log → Clearance log → Music cue sheet → Credits builder

    Archival leads turn into clearance items, and the music ends up on the cue sheet and in the credits.

  • Ready for E&O and delivery

    Clearance log → License tracker → Fair use log → Annotated-transcript clearance report → Chain-of-title binder

    License it, write up the fair use, annotate the script, and put the binder together.

  • Get the names right

    Subject tracker → Name supers list → Credits builder

    Spell someone’s name once, and it’s spelled the same in the supers and the credits.

Other free software we like

We're not building an editor or a sound mixer. Good free ones already exist, and we keep a list of the ones we've checked.

  • Editing: Kdenlive, Shotcut
  • Sound: Audacity, Ardour
  • Transcription: Buzz, noScribe
  • Subtitles: Subtitle Edit, Aegisub
  • Delivery: DCP-o-matic, HandBrake, MediaInfo

Help out

You don't need to write code.

  • Ask for a tool. Tell us about a job you do by hand.
  • Share a template. Take out anything private first.
  • Tell us what's broken. Something wrong or confusing. Please don't attach a real project file.

None of these tools send anything over the internet. Your project file stays on your computer unless you share it. This site doesn't use cookies or analytics.

The templates and forms are starting points, not legal advice. Have a lawyer look at anything you're going to sign. See Not legal advice.
