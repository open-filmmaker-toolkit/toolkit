## What's in each one

- **Subject tracker.** One row for each possible subject: who they are, how you found them, contact details, access status and the next follow-up.
- **Research and archival log.** One row for each archival lead or research source: what it is, where it's from, rights holder, contact, estimated cost, status and notes.
- **Comps tracker.** One row for each comparable film: year, running time, premiere, distributor, where it played, figures with their sources, tags and why it's comparable.
- **Treatment.** The sections of a treatment, from logline to team, each with a note on what goes in it and a typical word target.
- **Grant calendar.** One row for each grant or fund: funder, program, deadline, status, amounts, eligibility, what the application needs, and the dates you submitted and expect a decision.
- **Budget.** One row for each budget line: account, section, description, quantity, unit, multiplier, rate, fringe and total.
- **Finance plan.** One row for each source of money: kind, status, amount, terms and when the payments arrive.
- **Funder CRM.** One row for each funder, investor or broadcaster: kind, stage, the people there, the last contact, the next step and when it's due, and applications.
- **Cash flow.** One row for each month or week: money out, money in, the net and the running balance, and whether it's short.
- **Shooting calendar.** One row for each shoot day, travel day or hold: the trip, what you're filming, where, who's needed, and whether the call sheet is started.
- **Location scout sheet.** One row for each scout: the place, when and who, the verdict, who to call, and access, power, sound, light, parking, restrooms, signal and permits. The document is a sheet to print and fill in on the visit.
- **Deal memos.** One row for each crew deal memo: role, dates, rate, guarantee, kit fee, overtime, payment terms, credit and status. To sign one, use the deal memo form under legal forms.
- **Gear list.** One row for each piece of gear: department, how many, owned, borrowed or rented and from whom, day and week rates, replacement value, serial numbers and the days it's packed for.
- **Certificates and permits.** One row for each certificate of insurance or filming permit: who it's from, status, when you asked and got it, the dates it covers, who's named on it or its conditions, and the locations and shoot days it's for.
- **Travel sheet.** One row for each flight, train, drive, place to stay or local contact on a trip: dates and times, from and to, carrier or hotel, number, confirmation, address, phone and who it's for.
- **Risk assessment.** One row for each hazard: the plan it's in, likelihood and impact from 1 to 3, the rating (the two multiplied), what you'll do about it and who makes sure.
- **Security checklist.** One row for each item and person: the section, what to do, whether it's only for a high-risk shoot, and whether that person has done it.
- **Interview questions.** One row for each question, in order, for each interview: must-asks marked, the story beat it covers, and whether you asked it.
- **Grant narrative library.** The answers applications usually ask for, from logline to bios, each in typical lengths with a place to note the count, and a table of which funder got which version.
- **Pitch deck.** An outline with one heading per slide, from title and logline to contact, each with a note on what goes on it and a line for its images.
- **Camera and sound reports.** One row for each clip or sound file: the date and shoot day, camera or sound, the card, camera or recorder, operator, codec or format, frame rate, what or who, timecode, lens or tracks, whether it's circled, and where the card was copied.
- **Press list.** One row for each outlet: its kind and status, the people and their beats, screeners sent, RSVPs, coverage, website and notes.
- **Partner CRM.** One row for each partner organization: its kind and stage, the people, the screenings it's part of, the next step and open commitments, website and notes.
- **Interview moments.** One row for each moment in an interview, for the paper edit: who, when, where, which cards, the timecode, what they say, whether it's a favorite, the story beat, the release and the transcript.
- **Clearance log.** One row for each piece of third-party material: what it is, where it's used, who holds the rights, what you need, status and cost. The document adds a box for the film's details and a key to the types and statuses.
- **Release log.** One row for each release: what kind, who or what it covers, when it was signed and where the signed copy is. The document adds a list of everyone on camera and every place you film, so you can see who still needs a release. It's a log, not a release form.
- **Music cue sheet.** One row for each cue, in the order it plays, with timings, usage and the composers, authors and publishers with their society and share. The document adds the production details distributors ask for and a key to the usage and role codes.
- **Footage log.** One row for each clip: file name and folder, date, what's in it, who's in it, where, keywords, a rating from 1 to 5, comments, starting timecode, length and card. The column names are ones DaVinci Resolve's metadata import uses.
- **Paper edit.** One row for each bite, in the order of the edit: the scene, speaker, words, a note, the transcript and clip it's from, and its source and record timecodes.
- **Structure board.** One row for each scene card, in order: act, sequence, scene, summary, running time, story threads and notes.
- **Cut notes.** One row for each note on a cut: the cut, its date and running time, the screening and who saw it, the time or range in the cut, scene, shot, who gave the note, the note and its status.
- **Cost report.** One row for each budget line: section, account, budget, spent, estimate to complete, estimated final cost, variance and a note.
- **Name supers.** One row for each on-screen name and title, in the order they appear, with the timecode where each first appears.
- **End credits.** Credits in the usual order, with placeholders to replace, and music, archival, thanks and copyright lines. The document adds a table of the credits your contracts require, to tick off before you lock, and a checklist.

## Using them

- **Spreadsheets** (`.csv`) open in Excel, Numbers, Google Sheets (**File > Import**) and LibreOffice. Save a copy as `.xlsx` from there if you need that format.
- **Documents** (`.docx`) open in Word, Google Docs, Pages and LibreOffice. They're US Letter; switch to A4 in your page setup if you need to. To add a row to a table, put the cursor in the last cell and press Tab. To fill them in by hand, print them; the logs repeat their column headings on every page.
- **Same columns as the tools.** The subject tracker, research and archival log, comps tracker, grant calendar, budget, finance plan, funder CRM, cash flow, shooting calendar, interview questions, location scout sheet, deal memos, gear list, certificates and permits, travel sheet, risk assessment, security checklist, camera and sound reports, interview moments, press list, partner CRM, footage log, paper edit, structure board, cut notes, cost report, clearance log, release log, music cue sheet and name supers spreadsheets have the same columns, in the same order, as each tool's CSV export, and the call sheet matches the call sheet's spreadsheet export. Some exports add columns the tool works out for you, such as a cue's duration in seconds or what's still missing.

These templates are working records, not legal advice, and you use them at your own risk: see [Not legal advice](../README.md#not-legal-advice). Check what your distributor, broadcaster and E&O insurer need.

## Legal forms

Forms you sign, in [legal-forms](legal-forms/). They're under [CC0 1.0](../LICENSES/CC0-1.0.txt): no rights reserved, so you can turn one into your own contract without crediting us.

| Form | Document | Fill it in with |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Crew deal memo | [legal-forms/deal-memo-template.docx](legal-forms/deal-memo-template.docx) | [Deal memos](../tools/deal-memos/) |

Each form starts with a not-legal-advice notice and a "Written for" line. Fill in where you are, have a lawyer licensed there review the form, and only then delete the notice. Blank release forms aren't here yet.

## Contributing

Have a template that works better? Share it from the [new issue page](https://github.com/open-filmmaker-toolkit/toolkit/issues/new/choose) with **Submit a template**. Remove real names, contact details, rates and anything private first.

To contribute a legal form, say where it was written for and keep the not-legal-advice notice at the top; CI checks for both.

If you change a tool's CSV export, update its template here too. CI checks that each template's columns appear in the tool's export, in the same order. Edit the `.docx` files in Word or LibreOffice. Don't add macros, linked images or other external links: the validator rejects them.
