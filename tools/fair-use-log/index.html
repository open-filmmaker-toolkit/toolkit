      Fair use

      From the clearance log. Change these details there.

      1. Purpose and character of the use

      What does the film do with it that the original didn't? Is it the subject of comment or criticism, or used to illustrate a point the film makes? Is it there for its own sake, or as entertainment?

      2. Nature of the original work

      Is it factual, such as news footage or a document, or creative, such as a song or a film? Was it published?

      3. How much is used, and why

      How much of the original appears, and is that only as much as the film's purpose needs? Is it the heart of the work?

      4. Effect on the market for the original

      Could the film's use stand in for the original, or for a license the owner usually sells? Is there an established licensing market for this kind of use?

      Fair use is a US doctrine. Other countries have narrower exceptions, such as fair dealing in Canada, the UK and Australia, or none that fit, so a use that's fair in the US may need a license elsewhere. Ask your attorney about each country.
